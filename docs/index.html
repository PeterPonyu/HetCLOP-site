Abstract

When should a count decoder add dispersion?

Same body as the PeerJ PDF. Numbers match numbers.tex / the verifier.

Conditional single-cell generators are judged on how faithfully they reproduce within-cell-type heterogeneity, yet it is unclear when a conditional count decoder should model that heterogeneity by adding dispersion. We give a design rule: extra dispersion helps when the Poisson-on-mean draw (sampling each count from a Poisson distribution whose rate is the model’s predicted value for that cell and gene) leaves a within-type variance deficit, and the dispersion is conditional enough (a per-cell and/or per-type dispersion, as opposed to a single global value) to place that variance per group. The second condition binds in proportion to how heterogeneous the true dispersion is across cell types—with a homogeneous true dispersion a single global value suffices.

Systematic experiments across 50 datasets (133,845 cells) show that violating the rule carries a measurable biological cost. In mean-fixed re-decoding—holding a deployed model’s own fitted mean fixed and varying only the dispersion—a global, marginally-fitted dispersion, the default in deployed generators such as CFGen and scVI, recovers only 0.558 of marker genes (the per-cell-type top differentially-expressed genes whose recovery is the standard biological-content check) against 0.793 for a conditional one and more than halves rare-cell-type recovery, while carrying the best marginal variance calibration of the three draws.

Honest scope on the biological-cost claim: the conditional draw’s apparent “win” over Poisson rests on marker recall; on TSTR macro-F1 the gap 0.004 is smaller than its own across-seed standard deviation (0.008) and the two draws tie. End-to-end, with latents sampled from the fitted prior, the latent path dominates the error and the marker gap closes, but the rare-type harm survives in a narrower sense (0.052 against 0.078 for the dispersion-free draw).

On scVI, switching from global to per-cell amortised dispersion flips which decoder wins; a controlled probe holding the mean fixed isolates the parameterisation as the cause. The rule emerges from diagnosing why encoder choice—the usual lever—is not the bottleneck: a within-over-between tightness ratio used as a heterogeneity diagnostic matches the identity ceiling under a random projection (0.9994, and beats principal components on 8/8 datasets), and once circularity is broken, six non-degenerate encoders each seeing half of a 2,500-gene panel span only 0.0045 in reconstruction correlation. A standard single-cell UMAP embedding can under-report the dispersion failure severalfold, so the distortion is invisible where practitioners usually look. Every number reported in the prose and tables is machine-checked against the source artifacts.
